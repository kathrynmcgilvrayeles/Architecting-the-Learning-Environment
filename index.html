The Execution Mindset

Stop designing for understanding. Start designing for execution.

Knowledge and understanding remain critical foundations of any learning experience, but they are the starting line, not the finish line. We must deeply understand the specific skill being built and actively design to build the learner's confidence to apply it after completing the learning.

The true measure of our instructional design is not the final quiz score—it is whether the learner has the competence and self-assurance to execute the skills and behaviours required for the job.

Mapping the Execution Ecosystem

What specific behaviour are we wanting to change and see? Are we looking at a skills or cultural shift? This focuses squarely on the observable actions we need.

Does the actual work environment permit this behaviour? If the software is slow, the process is broken, or the physical tools are missing, the training will fail regardless of quality.

Do they need to memorize this, or can we provide a tool? Design job aids, checklists, and search-driven resources to support execution exactly when it is needed.

Will their manager support this new behaviour? Will their peers ridicule them for doing it differently? Social proof and leader modelling are often stronger than any formal training module.

How will the learner know they are executing correctly? Design mechanisms for immediate feedback, whether through system data, peer review, or coaching.

Architecting the Environment

Hackathons

Intensive, time-boxed collaborative events. They force teams to apply skills under pressure to solve real business problems, rapidly accelerating competence and teamwork.

Communities of Practice

Sustained, structured groups of practitioners who share a passion for a specific discipline. They learn how to execute better by sharing real-world challenges and solutions.

Champion Networks

Activating peer influencers to model the target behaviour. Social proof travels faster than formal training. Champions make the new behaviour safe, trusted, and normal.

Workflow Integration

Embedding tools, job aids, and micro-learnings directly into the systems where the work happens (e.g., Salesforce, Teams), ensuring support is available at the exact point of execution.
